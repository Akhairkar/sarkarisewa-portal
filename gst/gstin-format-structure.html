What you need to know

A GSTIN is a 15-character identifier. Understanding its structure can help you catch typing errors or obviously malformed numbers before you perform a taxpayer search.

How to check it

Use format checking as a first step only. A number can look correctly structured and still be wrong, cancelled or associated with different taxpayer details. Confirm the actual number through the official GST taxpayer-search facility.

What the result means

The standard GSTIN structure includes a two-digit state or union-territory code, a PAN-linked portion, entity-related characters, a default character and a checksum character. The format is useful for validation, but format alone does not prove that a GSTIN is registered or active.

Practical review tips

When checking an invoice, compare the GSTIN character-by-character with the supplier's records. Pay particular attention to commonly confused characters and accidental spaces or copied digits. Do not alter the GSTIN merely because a search result is unexpected.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is GSTIN verification the same as GST registration?

No. GST registration is the registration process, while GSTIN is the 15-character identifier associated with a GST registration record.

Is the SarkariSewa India verification service a government service?

No. It is an independent paid service. The official GST Portal remains the government reference for GST records and services.

Can a GSTIN format check prove that the number is active?

No. Format checking can identify obvious structural problems, but actual status and taxpayer details should be checked through the GST taxpayer-search facility.
